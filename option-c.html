About

I am a Senior Solutions Architect at GitLab, working with engineering teams in India and ASEAN on DevSecOps and agentic AI. Before that I spent three and a half years at AWS, first as a Technical Trainer and then as a Solutions Architect, after leading cloud security architecture at KPMG India.

The through-line is the same in every role: take a hard technical topic, work out what a team actually needs, and leave them able to run with it. That has meant architecture reviews and migrations, security programs, classroom teaching and, more recently, helping teams adopt AI safely.

Before consulting, my work was hands-on engineering. A 2017 post on the AWS Machine Learning Blog, a face-recognition service built with Rekognition, Lambda and DynamoDB, is from that period.

At KPMG India, as a Manager and Cloud Security Architect, I led cloud security architecture and posture work for global enterprise clients: zero trust, identity and compliance automation across AWS, Azure and Google Cloud.

At AWS I started as an Authorized Instructor, teaching architecture, security, machine learning and DevOps to hundreds of professionals, and earned all 13 AWS certifications along the way. As a Solutions Architect II I worked with enterprise customers in financial services, healthcare and technology on Well-Architected reviews, migration assessments and AI/ML designs, and ran Amazon Bedrock workshops for developer communities.

At GitLab I help enterprises move to DevSecOps and bring agentic AI into the software lifecycle with GitLab Duo Agent Platform. I also contribute upstream: two merge requests to GitLab are merged so far, alongside fixes to Strands Agents.

Outside the day job I speak, write and teach. That is 30+ talks, workshops and webcasts, articles on AWS Builder Center, tutorials on YouTube, a co-organizer role at AWS User Group Indore and a track co-host role at AWS Community Day Bengaluru.

How I work

  • Start from the constraint. The budget, the compliance rule and the team's skills shape the design more than the service catalogue does.
  • Write it down. Diagrams, decisions and runbooks outlast the meeting.
  • Teach as I go. An engagement should leave the team more capable than it found them.
  • Work in public. Talks, writing and upstream fixes keep my advice honest.

What I am thinking about

Four themes that run through my talks, articles and open-source work. Each one links to the proof.

Agents need approval gates, not just autonomy

The useful AI agent is the one you can trust with a change. Human approval on every state-changing action beats a clever demo.

Read Instanote

Guardrails are architecture, not an afterthought

Responsible GenAI is designed in from the first diagram: content filters, grounding and least-privilege access around the model.

Read the guardrails guide

Security shifts left, and now automates itself

Detection and remediation are moving into the developer workflow, from posture management to security agents that propose the fix.

Read about AWS Security Agent

GitOps is the delivery contract for the AI era

Declarative, pull-based delivery keeps platforms auditable as AI starts to write more of the change.

See the GitOps talk
